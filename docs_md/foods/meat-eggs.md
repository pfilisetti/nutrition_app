# Meat and Eggs

---

## Eggs

- **Raw vs cooked**: Cooked recommended
- **Why cook**: Protein digestibility increases from 51% (raw) to 91% (cooked). Heat unfolds protein structures, making them easier to digest. Raw eggs carry Salmonella risk and contain avidin, a protein that causes biotin deficiency by blocking biotin absorption.
- **Best method**: Poach or boil to limit air exposure and maximize nutrient retention. Do not overcook.
- **Benefits**: Eggs increase HDL ("good" cholesterol), reducing heart disease and stroke risk. Excellent source of choline (cell membranes, brain signaling). Lutein and zeaxanthin protect against cataracts and macular degeneration.

**Key nutrients**:

## Beef

- **Why eat**: One of the few foods that reliably covers 100% B12 in a single serving. Good heme iron source (well absorbed compared to plant iron).
- **Note**: Limit red meat to 1–3 portions per week. Prefer lean cuts. Grass-fed beef contains 300–500% more CLA than grain-fed.

**Key nutrients**:

| Nutrient | Amount | % Daily Value |
|---------|--------|---------------|
| Vitamin B12 | 2.4 mcg (per 85g) | ~100% |
| Iron (heme) | 2.7 mg (per 100g) | ~15% |

## Liver

- **Why eat**: Exceptionally nutrient-dense — the highest natural source of vitamin A, B12, iron, and copper. Often called a "superfood" for micronutrient density.
- **Caution**: Very high in preformed vitamin A (retinol) — do not consume more than once per week (risk of vitamin A toxicity). Avoid during pregnancy (excess retinol is teratogenic).
- **Key nutrients**: vitamin A (extremely high), vitamin B12, iron, copper, folate, zinc, protein
