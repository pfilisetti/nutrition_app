# Proteins

## What Are Proteins?

Proteins are the main building blocks of the body. They make up muscles, tendons, organs, and skin, and are essential for enzymes, hormones, neurotransmitters, and immune function. The name comes from the Greek "proteos" meaning "primary" or "first place."

Proteins are chains of amino acids linked by peptide bonds. 20 amino acids exist — the body produces some, but 9 must come from diet (essential amino acids).

**Energy**: 4 calories per gram (same as carbohydrates; fat provides 9 cal/g)

---

## Daily Protein Intake

| Goal | Intake |
|------|--------|
| Minimum | 0.8 g/kg body weight |
| Muscle building / maintenance | 1.4–2.0 g/kg body weight |
| Per meal | 20–30g |

Proteins are NOT stored for later use. Excess protein is converted to glucose or triglycerides for energy or fat storage. If insufficient protein is consumed, the body breaks down muscle tissue (muscle wasting).

## Essential Amino Acids (9 total)

| Amino Acid | Key Function |
|-----------|--------------|
| **Leucine** | Critical for protein synthesis, muscle repair, blood sugar regulation, wound healing, growth hormone production. Look for leucine-rich protein sources for muscle building. |
| **Isoleucine** | Muscle metabolism; concentrated in muscle tissue; immune function, hemoglobin production, energy regulation |
| **Valine** | Muscle growth and regeneration; energy production; one of 3 BCAAs (branched-chain amino acids) |
| **Lysine** | Protein synthesis, calcium absorption, hormone and enzyme production, energy, immune function, collagen and elastin production |
| **Threonine** | Structural component of collagen and elastin (skin, connective tissue); fat metabolism; immune function |
| **Tryptophan** | Precursor to serotonin (mood, sleep, appetite regulation); also converted to niacin (B3) — requires iron, B6, and B2 for conversion. Found in chicken, eggs, cheese, peanuts, fish, milk. |
| **Methionine** | Metabolism and detoxification; tissue growth; absorption of zinc and selenium |
| **Phenylalanine** | Precursor to tyrosine, dopamine, epinephrine, norepinephrine; structural component of proteins and enzymes |
| **Histidine** | Produces histamine (immune response, digestion, sleep-wake cycles); maintains myelin sheath (nerve cell protective barrier) |

**BCAAs (Branched-Chain Amino Acids)**: Leucine, isoleucine, valine — particularly important for muscle metabolism.

## Types of Proteins by Function

### Structural Proteins
- Provide structure, stiffness, and elasticity to cells and tissues
- **Collagen**: Most abundant protein in the body; structural component of bones, tendons, ligaments, and skin
- **Keratin**: Found in skin, nails, and hair
- **Elastin**: Several hundred times more flexible than collagen; allows tissues to return to original shape after stretching (uterus, lungs, arteries)

### Enzymes
- Aid thousands of biochemical reactions inside and outside cells
- Combine with substrates (other molecules) to catalyze reactions essential to metabolism
- Examples: digestive enzymes (lactase, sucrase break down sugars), enzymes for energy production, blood clotting, muscle contraction
- Some require vitamins or minerals as cofactors to function

### Hormonal Proteins
- Made and secreted by endocrine tissues
- Key examples:
  - **Insulin**: signals glucose uptake into cells
  - **Glucagon**: signals liver to release stored glycogen
  - **hGH** (human growth hormone): stimulates tissue and bone growth
  - **ADH** (antidiuretic hormone): regulates water retention
  - **ACTH**: stimulates cortisol release (key for metabolism)

### Transport Proteins
- Carry substances (nutrients, gases, cholesterol) through the bloodstream
- **Hemoglobin**: carries oxygen from lungs to body tissues
- **GLUT transporters**: move glucose to cells
- **Lipoproteins**: transport cholesterol and fats in blood (see other-compounds.md)
- Transport proteins are highly specific — a glucose transporter will not carry cholesterol

### Storage Proteins
- **Ferritin**: stores iron
- **Casein**: principal protein in milk; helps infants grow

### Antibodies (Immunoglobulins)
- Immune defense proteins that identify and neutralize foreign pathogens

### Fluid Balance Proteins
- **Albumin** and **globulin**: proteins in blood that attract and retain water, maintaining fluid balance between blood vessels and tissue
- Severe protein deficiency → albumin/globulin levels drop → fluid leaks into tissues → **kwashiorkor** (a form of severe protein malnutrition)

## Protein Quality and Food Sources

- **Complete proteins** (all 9 essential amino acids): lean meats, poultry, fish, eggs, dairy, and some plant sources (quinoa, soy)
- **Plant proteins**: generally lower in certain essential amino acids; combining different plant sources (grains + legumes) achieves complete amino acid coverage
- Animal protein provides higher bioavailability — fiber in plants slightly reduces absorption of plant protein

**Best muscle-building sources**: High in protein, low in saturated fat, rich in leucine. Examples: chicken breast, Greek yogurt, eggs, lentils, fish.

**Fiber and plant proteins**: Fiber in plant foods slightly reduces protein absorption compared to animal sources.
