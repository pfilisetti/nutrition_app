# Omega-3 and Dietary Fats

## Types of Dietary Fat

- **Unsaturated fats** (healthy): monounsaturated (MUFA) and polyunsaturated (PUFA), including omega-3 and omega-6
- **Saturated fats**: more stable at high temperatures but should be consumed in moderation (see fats-and-oils.md for cooking implications)

---

## Omega-3 Fatty Acids

Three main types of omega-3:
- **ALA** (alpha-linolenic acid): plant-based; must be converted by the body to EPA and DHA — conversion is inefficient
- **EPA** (eicosapentaenoic acid): marine-based; anti-inflammatory
- **DHA** (docosahexaenoic acid): marine-based; critical for brain and eye health

---

## Foods High in Omega-3 (Unsaturated Fats)

### Chia Seeds
- **Fat content**: 9g total fat, only 1g saturated
- **Omega-3**: 5,050 mg ALA per oz (28g)
- **Note**: Most omega-3 in chia is ALA — the body must convert it to EPA/DHA, which is inefficient. Pair with direct EPA/DHA sources.

### Salmon
- **Omega-3**: 2,150 mg EPA + DHA (combined) per 3.5 oz (100g)
- Direct source of EPA and DHA — no conversion needed

### Herring
- **Omega-3**: 2,150 mg EPA + DHA (combined) per 3.5 oz (100g)
- Direct source of EPA and DHA

### Flax Seeds (Graines de Lin)
- **Omega-3**: 2,350 mg ALA per tablespoon (10.3g) of whole seeds
- Plant-based ALA source — same conversion limitation as chia

### Brussels Sprouts
- **Omega-3**: 44 mg ALA per half-cup (44g) raw; 135 mg ALA per half-cup (78g) cooked (3x more when cooked)
- Low amount compared to fish or seeds — a complementary source

---

## Foods High in Saturated Fat

### Coconut Milk
- **Fat type**: Mostly saturated; ~55% medium-chain triglycerides (MCTs)
- **MCTs**: Stimulate energy through thermogenesis (heat production); harder for the body to convert into stored fat; easier to burn off than long-chain triglycerides (LCTs)
- **Key compound**: Lauric acid — coconut oil is ~42% lauric acid, one of the best natural sources. Lauric acid has antimicrobial and anti-inflammatory properties and may support the immune system.
- **Note**: Coconut milk is not naturally occurring — made by mixing coconut flesh with water (~50% water). Coconut water is ~94% water with much less fat and fewer nutrients.
- **vs. Dairy**: Dairy sources tend to have a higher proportion of capra fatty acids and lower lauric acid than coconut oil.
