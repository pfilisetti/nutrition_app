# Carbohydrates and Sugars

## Overview

Carbohydrates are made up of three components: fiber, starch, and sugar.

**Added sugar limits**:
- WHO: max 5% of daily calories from added/free sugars (~30g / 7 teaspoons for adults)
- USDA: max 10% of total calories (~50g / 12 teaspoons on a 2,000-calorie diet)

White sugar (sucrose) has had virtually all fiber, phytochemicals, vitamins, and minerals removed.

---

## Simple Carbohydrates

Monosaccharides (1 sugar molecule) or disaccharides (2 sugar molecules). Digested quickly and release sugars rapidly into the bloodstream.

### Glucose (Blood Sugar)
- Primary energy source for every cell in the body, especially the brain
- The brain's constant need for glucose is why the minimum recommended daily carbohydrate intake is 130g for adults
- When blood glucose rises, the pancreas releases insulin to signal cells to absorb glucose
- Also triggers release of leptin (appetite suppressor) and ghrelin (appetite increaser)
- Low-GI foods suppress ghrelin, helping regulate satiety
- GI value: 100 (the reference point)

### Fructose (Fruit Sugar)
- Naturally occurring in fruit, honey, sucrose, and high-fructose corn syrup
- ~1.5x sweeter than sucrose
- Absorbed directly into the bloodstream; does NOT trigger insulin or raise blood glucose directly
- GI value: ~19
- **Metabolized entirely by the liver** — excess fructose gets converted to fat (lipogenesis), can lead to nonalcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD), visceral fat, insulin resistance, and increased triglycerides
- One of the most damaging forms of sugar in excess
- **Important**: Do not give up fruit — fructose from whole fruit is a minor intake for most people and comes with fiber, vitamins, and antioxidants that offset the effect

### Sucrose (Table Sugar)
- Disaccharide: 50% glucose + 50% fructose
- GI value: 65
- Rapidly broken down into glucose and fructose; the fructose portion is metabolized by the liver (see fructose above)

### Lactose (Milk Sugar)
- Disaccharide: glucose + galactose
- Broken down by the enzyme lactase; lactose intolerance = inability to produce lactase
- GI value: ~41 (low — whole milk is a low-GI food)

### Dextrose
- Chemically identical to glucose; made from corn or wheat
- High-GI; raises blood sugar rapidly
- Used as artificial sweetener, food preservative, and in medical IV solutions

### Galactose
- Same chemical formula as glucose but different spatial configuration
- Cannot be used directly by glycolysis enzymes; must first be converted to glucose-6-phosphate via a multi-step process in cells

## Complex Carbohydrates

Composed of multiple simple sugars joined by chemical bonds. The more chains and branches, the more complex — and the longer digestion takes → less impact on blood sugar.

Include starches and dietary fiber.

**Benefits**: Key to long-term health; help maintain healthy weight; protect against type 2 diabetes and cardiovascular disease.

**Examples**: Whole grains (oats, brown rice, spelt, rye, barley)

---

## Starch (Amidon)

A complex carbohydrate. 4 types of starch (multiple types can co-exist in the same food):
- **Type 1**: Bound within fibrous cell walls — found in grains, seeds, legumes
- **Type 2**: Raw potatoes, green (unripe) bananas
- **Type 3 (Resistant Starch)**: Formed when starchy foods are cooked then cooled (retrogradation) — acts like soluble fermentable fiber; improves insulin sensitivity, lowers blood sugar, reduces appetite
- **Type 4**: Man-made, formed by chemical processing

Resistant starch has 2 cal/g vs 4 cal/g for regular starch. See fiber.md for more detail.

## Glycemic Index (GI)

A ranking of how quickly foods raise blood sugar levels after eating. High-GI foods are rapidly broken down into glucose.

- Glucose = 100 (reference)
- High GI: quickly broken down → rapid blood sugar spike → insulin surge → potential fat storage
- Low GI: slower digestion → steadier blood sugar → better satiety and energy

---

## Fruit Consumption Timing

- **Before a meal**: Eaten on an empty stomach, fruit is digested quickly → fast sugar absorption → energy spike → if not used physically, stored as fat; can cause hypoglycemia in sensitive individuals (pancreas over-secretes insulin)
- **Up to 20 min after a meal**: Stomach has begun digestion; the food mass (chyme) slows down sugar absorption — safer
- **20–40 min after a meal**: Digestion has started but fruit timing becomes less optimal
- **40+ min after a meal**: Fruit is processed more slowly alongside the meal — generally fine

## Sugar Alternatives

### Honey
- Composition: ~40% fructose, ~30% glucose, water, minerals (iron, calcium, potassium, magnesium)
- GI: ~55 (moderate); some varieties lower due to higher fructose content
- Richer in calories than sugar per teaspoon; darker varieties contain flavonoids (antibacterial, anti-inflammatory, antioxidant)
- **No real advantage for diabetics** — still affects blood sugar; classified as a free sugar
- Sweeter than table sugar → can use less

### Agave Syrup
- ~1.5x sweeter than sugar (use less to achieve same sweetness)
- GI: ~15 (very low) — absorbed slowly, minimal insulin spike
- **Warning**: Very high in fructose — processed almost entirely by the liver; similar issues as excess fructose
- If choosing agave: look for ethical brands processing at low temperatures; check fructose content is ~50% (some brands go up to 90%)

### Xylitol
- GI: 12 (very low); 2.4 cal/g; slowly absorbed; partly metabolized by the liver
- Benefits: dental health (inhibits tooth decay)
- **Warning**: Laxative effect in excess — may cause digestive discomfort or worsen IBS symptoms
- Frequently found in chewing gum and mints
