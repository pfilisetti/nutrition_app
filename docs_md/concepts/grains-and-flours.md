# Grains and Flours

## Grain Structure

Grains are the seeds of grass-like plants called cereals (corn, rice, wheat, etc.). Seeds of non-grass plants (pseudocereals) such as buckwheat, quinoa, and amaranth are also considered whole grains.

A whole grain has 3 parts — all must be present in their original proportion:
- **Bran** (outer shell): contains fiber, minerals, and antioxidants
- **Endosperm** (middle layer): mostly carbohydrates (starch) and a bit of protein
- **Germ** (inner layer): vitamins, minerals, protein, antioxidants, and plant compounds — the embryo of the plant

**Refined grains** have had the bran and germ removed, leaving only the endosperm. This removes most fiber, vitamins, minerals, and phenolic acids.

---

## Whole Grains vs Refined Grains

- Refined grains are digested quickly → blood sugar spikes → associated with poor blood sugar management
- Whole grains contain more phenolic acids (phytochemicals) — act as antioxidants with anti-inflammatory and anticancer effects
- Eating a lot of refined grains increases markers of inflammation (e.g., C-reactive protein), linked to heart disease and type 2 diabetes
- When buying processed whole-grain products: read the ingredient list to ensure made entirely from whole grains, not a mixture

## Oats

- **Type**: Whole grain; one of the healthiest whole grains
- **Key nutrients**: beta-glucan (soluble fiber), avenanthramide (antioxidant), avenalin (protein — 80% of total, not found in any other grain)
- **Benefits**:
  - Rich in beta-glucans: a soluble fiber that aids digestion and nutrient absorption; forms a gel-like solution at low concentration
  - 28 studies found beta-glucan-rich diets lower LDL and total cholesterol, and help lower blood sugar
  - Avenanthramide is a unique antioxidant
- **Fiber**: Majority is soluble (beta-glucan)

## Rice

- **Brown rice** (whole grain): 1.8g fiber per 100g cooked; contains lignans (antioxidants that reduce heart disease risk — lower blood pressure, inflammation, LDL cholesterol)
- **White rice** (refined): 0.6g fiber per 100g cooked; bran and germ removed

---

## Wheat (Whole Wheat Flour)

- **Key nutrients**: protein (7–22% of dry weight), fiber (12–15% of dry weight), zinc, manganese, phosphorus
- **Main fiber**: arabinoxylan (70% of bran fiber, a type of hemicellulose); also cellulose
- **Note**: Both whole and white wheat rank high on the glycemic index (GI) — unsuitable for people with diabetes. Most wheat fiber is insoluble.
- **White wheat**: Relatively poor in vitamins and minerals (bran and germ removed)
- **Antinutrients**: Contains phytic acid — soaking can reduce this
- **Tip**: Many whole-wheat breads are made from pulverized wheat, which reduces the benefits of whole grains. Choose breads with visible grains or seeds.

---

## Spelt (Épeautre)

- **Nutrients**: Rich in manganese (92% DV per 200g cooked), magnesium, phosphorus, zinc, iron, B vitamins, fiber; slightly more zinc and protein than whole wheat
- **Protein**: ~80% is gluten
- **Antinutrients**: Contains phytic acid (reduces zinc and iron absorption) — can reduce by sprouting, fermenting, or soaking. Greater concern for vegetarians and vegans.
- **Note**: Nutritional profile similar to common whole wheat (fiber and protein content)

## Quinoa

- **Type**: Pseudocereal (seed, not a true cereal grain)
- **Key nutrients**: Complete protein (all 9 essential amino acids); antioxidants (quercetin, kaempferol)
- **Benefits**: One of the few plants providing complete proteins; high antioxidant content; high in fiber
- **Note**: Nutritionally similar to cereal grains and consumed the same way

---

## Corn / Maize (Popcorn)

- **Key nutrients**: Manganese, magnesium, zinc, copper, phosphorus, potassium, B vitamins, lutein, zeaxanthin
- **Benefits**: Lutein and zeaxanthin are antioxidants linked to lower risk of macular degeneration and cataracts
- **Popcorn**: A whole-grain food; one of the healthiest snack options; 14.5g fiber per 100g (58% DV)

---

## Pasta

- **Standard pasta**: Made from durum wheat; refined
- **Whole-grain pasta**: 2.5x more fiber than regular; higher in fiber, manganese, and selenium; fewer calories due to fewer carbs
- **Important note**: Whole-wheat pasta is made from pulverized whole-wheat flour, which diminishes the benefits of intact whole grains. Whole-grain pasta is not as beneficial as intact whole grains like quinoa or brown rice.

---

## Coconut Flour

- **Source**: Byproduct of coconut milk and oil production; dried and ground coconut flesh
- **Key nutrients**: Very high in fiber (mostly insoluble), potassium, iron, plant-based protein
- **Benefits**: May improve blood sugar stability, digestion, and heart health; may reduce cholesterol and triglyceride levels
- **Comparison to wheat flour**: Higher in fiber, protein, potassium, and iron

## Almond Flour

- **Source**: Blanched almonds (skins removed) ground into fine flour
- **Key nutrients**: Very high in vitamin E (antioxidant), magnesium; low in carbs; high in healthy fats and fiber
- **Benefits**: Reduces LDL cholesterol and insulin resistance; lower glycemic impact; no phytic acid (lost in blanching process)
- **Note**: Different from almond meal (which is made with skins intact, resulting in coarser texture). Baked goods tend to be more flat and dense than with wheat flour.
- **Magnesium note**: 25–38% of people with type 2 diabetes have a magnesium deficiency — almond flour is a good source

## Comparison: Almond vs Coconut vs Brown Rice Flour

- Almond flour: higher in vitamins and minerals, higher in fat
- Coconut flour: higher in fiber; fewer calories per ounce than almond flour
- Brown rice flour: lighter option, more neutral flavor
