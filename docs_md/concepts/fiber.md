# Fiber

## What Is Fiber?

Fiber is a type of carbohydrate the body cannot digest. Unlike most carbs (broken down into glucose), fiber passes through the body largely undigested. It comes in two main categories: soluble and insoluble.

**Key facts**:
- Gut bacteria (gut flora) outnumber body cells 10 to 1 — in that sense, you're only 10% human
- Most foods feed only 10% of your cells; fermentable fibers and resistant starches feed the other 90% (the gut flora)
- Fiber regulates blood sugar, keeps hunger in check, promotes bowel regularity, and feeds beneficial gut bacteria

**Daily intake**: 25g (women) to 38g (men); of which 6–8g should be soluble (~1/4 of total)

**Tip**: Increase fiber intake gradually — sudden increases cause abdominal cramps, bloating, and diarrhea. Drink plenty of water.

Two types of fiber products:
- **Dietary fiber**: naturally present in foods
- **Functional fiber**: extracted and added to processed foods

---

## Soluble Fiber

- Dissolves in water
- Forms a gel-like substance in the gut that slows digestion and blocks fat absorption
- Examples: gums, pectins, psyllium, beta-glucans, inulin, oligofructose
- Found in: fruits, oatmeal, chia seeds, nuts, beans, lentils, apples, blueberries

**Benefits**:
- Lowers LDL cholesterol (traps cholesterol so it's excreted rather than absorbed)
- Reduces blood sugar spikes
- Increases feelings of fullness
- Reaches gut bacteria mostly intact → they ferment it into short-chain fatty acids (energy)

---

## Insoluble Fiber

- Does not dissolve in water
- Passes through the digestive system mostly intact
- Absorbs fluid and sticks to waste products → forms stool and speeds up transit
- Examples: lignin, cellulose
- Found in: whole wheat products (especially wheat bran), quinoa, brown rice, legumes, leafy greens (kale), almonds, walnuts, seeds, fruits with edible skins (pears, apples)

**Benefits**:
- Prevents constipation and intestinal blockages
- Reduces risk of hemorrhoids and colorectal cancer
- Promotes feelings of fullness by physically filling stomach space
- No calories (not digested at all)

---

## Fermentable Fiber (Prebiotic Fiber)

- Fiber that gut bacteria can digest and use as fuel
- Most fermentable fibers are soluble
- Examples: pectins, beta-glucans, guar gum, inulin, oligofructose
- Best food sources: beans and legumes

**Benefits**:
- Increases number and diversity of friendly gut bacteria (gut microbiome)
- Gut bacteria produce short-chain fatty acids (SCFAs) — butyric, propionic, acetic acid — with powerful health benefits for colon health, immunity, and metabolism

**Note**: A by-product of fermentation is gas — high-fermentable-fiber foods can cause flatulence and discomfort, especially if not accustomed to high fiber intake.

---

## Viscous Fiber

- A subset of soluble fiber that forms a thick gel when mixed with water
- "Sits" in the gut and dramatically slows digestion and nutrient absorption
- Examples: glucomannan, beta-glucans, pectins, guar gum, psyllium
- Found in: legumes, asparagus, Brussels sprouts, oats, flax seeds

**Benefits**:
- The most effective type of fiber for weight loss — reduces appetite and food intake
- Prolongs feelings of fullness
- The more viscous, the more effective at reducing hunger

---

## Resistant Starch

- A type of starch that resists digestion — passes through to the large intestine unchanged
- Classified as fiber (though technically starch)
- Functions like soluble, fermentable fiber in the gut
- Found in: raw potatoes, green (unripe) bananas, cashews, raw oats, cooled cooked potatoes and rice (retrogradation)
- Has fewer calories than regular starch (2 vs 4 calories per gram)

**4 types**:
- **Type 1**: found in grains, seeds, legumes — bound within fibrous cell walls
- **Type 2**: found in raw potatoes, green bananas
- **Type 3**: formed when starchy foods (potatoes, rice) are cooked then cooled — digestible starch converts to resistant starch via retrogradation
- **Type 4**: man-made, formed chemically

**Benefits**:
- Improves insulin sensitivity
- Lowers blood sugar after meals (and has a "second meal effect" — also lowers blood sugar at the next meal)
- Reduces appetite
- Feeds friendly gut bacteria → increases butyrate production → improves colon cell health
- Feeds beneficial gut bacteria, improving both microbiome diversity and function

**Note**: Ripening a banana (turning yellow) degrades resistant starch into regular starch. Allowing bananas to ripen fully reduces resistant starch content significantly.

---

## Cellulose (Insoluble Fiber)

- Present in all plant cell walls
- Also used as a food additive (extracted from wood or plant waste — oat hulls, peanut/almond skins)
- Generally considered safe; may be viscous or fermentable depending on form

---

## Fiber and Cholesterol

Soluble fiber helps lower LDL cholesterol through two mechanisms:
1. Forms a gel that traps dietary cholesterol → excreted as waste before reaching arteries
2. Binds to bile acids → forces the liver to make more bile acids using blood cholesterol → lowers circulating LDL

Insoluble fiber promotes weight loss by filling the stomach, crowding out cholesterol-raising foods.

## Best Sources of Fiber by Type

| Type | Best Sources |
|------|-------------|
| Soluble | Oats, apples, beans, lentils, chia seeds, blueberries |
| Insoluble | Wheat bran, whole grains, leafy greens, nuts, seeds |
| Fermentable | Beans, legumes, onions, garlic, Jerusalem artichoke |
| Viscous | Oats, flax seeds, legumes, asparagus |
| Resistant starch | Raw/cooled potatoes, green bananas, raw oats, cashews |
