# Vitamins

## General Principles

There are 13 essential vitamins. No single food provides all of them — variety is key.

**Fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K)**
- Stored in body tissue for later use
- Most abundant in high-fat foods; much better absorbed when eaten with fat
- These vitamins work together: optimizing vitamin A and K intake is important when supplementing vitamin D3

**Water-soluble vitamins (B group, C)**
- Not generally stored in the body (exception: B12, stored in the liver)
- Must be consumed regularly

Nutrients rarely work in isolation — increased intake of one may increase the need for another.

---

## Vitamin A (Retinoids)

- **Type**: Fat-soluble
- **Daily intake**: 700 mcg (max 3,000 mcg)
- **Functions**: Vision, formation and maintenance of teeth, bones, skin, and soft tissue; immune system support; provitamins are antioxidants
- **Forms in food**:
  - Preformed vitamin A (retinol, retinyl esters): found only in animal products
  - Provitamin A (carotenoids): abundant in plants — must be converted to retinol, then to active forms (retinal, retinoic acid)
- **Risk groups**: Vegans — provitamin A conversion to retinol is not always efficient and depends on genetics. Deficiency is common in developing countries; globally, it is the most common cause of preventable blindness.

---

## Vitamin B1 (Thiamine)

- **Type**: Water-soluble
- **Daily intake**: 1.1 mg
- **Functions**: Converts carbohydrates into usable energy; essential cofactor for enzymes involved in glucose, amino acid, and lipid metabolism
- **Note**: Side effects of deficiency include buildup of pyruvic acid in the bloodstream (inability to convert food into fuel)

---

## Vitamin B2 (Riboflavin)

- **Type**: Water-soluble
- **Daily intake**: 1.1 mg
- **Functions**: Produces red blood cells; supports energy production and metabolism (fats, proteins, carbohydrates); cellular function and growth
- **Note**: Essential component of coenzymes FMN and FAD. Required for converting tryptophan to niacin (B3). Light-sensitive — reason milk is not stored in glass containers.
- **Risk groups**: People who don't consume dairy; vegans and vegetarians

---

## Vitamin B3 (Niacin)

- **Type**: Water-soluble
- **Daily intake**: 14 mg NE (1 NE = 1 mg niacin or 60 mg tryptophan)
- **Functions**: Skin and nervous system health; energy production; cellular function; antioxidant properties
- **Note**: Over 400 enzymes require NAD (derived from niacin) — more than any other vitamin-derived coenzyme. The body can also synthesize small amounts from the amino acid tryptophan. People deficient in iron, riboflavin, or B6 are at higher risk of deficiency.

---

## Vitamin B5 (Pantothenic Acid)

- **Type**: Water-soluble
- **Daily intake**: 5 mg
- **Functions**: Metabolism, production of hormones, converts food into energy; essential for synthesis of Coenzyme A (CoA), which is involved in fatty acid synthesis and degradation
- **Note**: Deficiency is extremely rare — pantothenic acid is found in virtually all foods

---

## Vitamin B6 (Pyridoxine)

- **Type**: Water-soluble
- **Daily intake**: 1.3 mg
- **Functions**: Healthy brain function; red blood cell formation; cognitive development; neurotransmitter biosynthesis; amino acid, carbohydrate, and lipid metabolism; may help with depression
- **Active forms**: Pyridoxal 5' phosphate (PLP) and pyridoxamine 5' phosphate (PMP)

---

## Vitamin B7 (Biotin)

- **Type**: Water-soluble
- **Daily intake**: 30 mcg
- **Functions**: Metabolizes proteins and carbohydrates; supports hair and skin; plays key roles in histone modifications, gene regulation, and cell signaling; catalyzes critical steps in fatty acid, glucose, and amino acid metabolism

---

## Vitamin B9 (Folate / Folic Acid)

- **Type**: Water-soluble
- **Daily intake**: 400 mcg DFE
- **Functions**: Red blood cell formation; DNA synthesis
- **Note**: "Folate" = generic name for all forms of B9; "folic acid" = synthetic form. At least 85% of folic acid is bioavailable when taken with food, vs ~50% for natural food folate. Deficiency rarely occurs alone — usually associated with poor diet and other deficiencies. Produces anemia indistinguishable from B12-deficiency anemia.
- **DFE conversion** (Dietary Folate Equivalents):
  - 1 mcg DFE = 1 mcg food folate
  - 1 mcg DFE = 0.6 mcg folic acid from fortified foods (taken with food)
  - 1 mcg DFE = 0.5 mcg folic acid taken on an empty stomach

---

## Vitamin B12 (Cobalamin)

- **Type**: Water-soluble (exception: stored in the liver — 1 to 5 mg stored)
- **Daily intake**: 2.4 mcg
- **Functions**: Central nervous system maintenance; metabolism; red blood cell formation; DNA synthesis; prevents anemia
- **Note**: The only vitamin containing a metallic element (cobalt). Found naturally only in animal products. Deficiency symptoms may take a long time to appear due to liver storage. Over 20% of older adults may be deficient (absorption decreases with age).
- **Risk groups**: Vegans, vegetarians, older adults

---

## Vitamin C (L-Ascorbic Acid)

- **Type**: Water-soluble
- **Daily intake**: 75 mg
- **Functions**: Maintains healthy tissue; dental health; wound healing; powerful antioxidant; reduces uric acid levels; boosts immunity; supports memory and cognitive function; essential for collagen biosynthesis; improves absorption of non-heme iron; regenerates other antioxidants including vitamin E
- **Note**: The body cannot produce vitamin C — must come entirely from diet

---

## Vitamin D (Calciferol)

- **Type**: Fat-soluble
- **Daily intake**: 15 mcg (600 IU)
- **Functions**: Enables calcium absorption in the gut; builds healthy teeth and bones; maintains phosphate concentrations for bone mineralization; prevents hypocalcemic tetany
- **Forms**:
  - D2 (ergocalciferol): found in plants, mushrooms, yeasts
  - D3 (cholecalciferol): found in fatty fish and egg yolks; produced in skin from sunlight exposure; almost twice as effective as D2 at raising blood vitamin D levels
- **Synthesis**: Produced from cholesterol in the skin upon sun exposure → converted to calcidiol (storage form) in the liver → converted to calcitriol (active form) in the kidneys
- **Interactions**: Magnesium is important for vitamin D function; fat-soluble vitamins A and K should be optimized when supplementing D3
- **Deficiency**: Very common — ~42% of Americans may be deficient; rises to 74% in older adults and 82% in people with dark skin

---

## Vitamin E

- **Type**: Fat-soluble
- **Daily intake**: 15 mg (max 1,000 mg)
- **Functions**: Organ function; antioxidant properties; protects cell membranes against oxidative damage
- **Note**: A group of eight fat-soluble compounds; alpha-tocopherol best meets human dietary requirements

---

## Vitamin K

- **Type**: Fat-soluble
- **Daily intake**: 90 mcg
- **Functions**: Blood clotting (coagulation); regulates calcium deposition; activates proteins critical for blood clotting, heart health, and bone health
- **Forms**:
  - K1 (phylloquinone): primarily in green leafy vegetables; ~75–90% of all vitamin K consumed; transported to and used by the liver
  - K2 (menaquinones, MK-4 through MK-13): found in animal-based and fermented foods; also produced by gut bacteria; circulates in blood longer than K1 (days vs hours), allowing better use in tissues throughout the body
