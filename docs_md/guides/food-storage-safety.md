# Food Storage and Safety

## Defrosting

### General rules
- Always defrost at low temperature (fridge or cold running water)
- Never defrost on the counter — bacteria multiply rapidly at room temperature
- You can re-freeze partially defrosted food only if it still has ice crystals on its surface
- Break up pieces of frozen food before defrosting to speed up the process
- Consume defrosted food immediately after thawing

### Meat, Fish, and Seafood

**Recommended methods** (safest to least safe):
1. **In the refrigerator** — safest method. Allow 24 hours per 2.5 kg (5 lbs) of poultry, or 24 hours for most other meats.
2. **Cold water in a sealed package** — wrap tightly in leak-proof plastic, submerge fully in cold water, change water every 30 minutes.
3. **Microwave** — only if cooking immediately after defrosting.

**Important**: If meat is still partly frozen when you cook it, it will cook unevenly and may not reach the safe internal temperature needed to kill bacteria. Cook immediately after defrosting.

### Vegetables and Fruit

**Method**: Cold running water. Can be defrosted directly without the fridge.

### Dairy (Milk, Yoghurt, Butter)

**Do not freeze if already opened.** These should not be frozen once opened.

### Eggs

Eggs and egg-based foods must be cooked to an internal temperature of at least 74°C (165°F).

## Reheating

### General safety rules
- Do not leave cooked food at room temperature for more than 2 hours (USDA guideline)
- The "danger zone" for bacterial growth: 4–60°C (40–140°F)
- It's safe to refrigerate hot leftovers immediately — no need to wait for them to cool
- For large dishes (soup, stew), divide into small shallow containers before refrigerating
- Store leftovers in airtight containers

### Foods that must not be reheated (or only once, carefully)

**Spinach, celery, beets, carrots, and leafy greens** — high in nitrates, which convert to toxic nitrites and potentially carcinogenic nitrosamines when reheated at high heat. If reheating spinach: heat in a covered container to a minimum of 70°C for at least 2 minutes. Do not reheat more than once.

**Mushrooms** — reheat within 1 day maximum, and only once.

**Potatoes and rice** — do not reheat in the microwave after initial cooking.

**Chicken and eggs** — do not reheat in the microwave. Egg-based dishes must be reheated to at least 70°C. Never microwave a hard-boiled egg (risk of explosion and burns).

**Oils** — reheating oils repeatedly causes them to form harmful fatty acid bonds and increases trans fat content, contributing to inflammation and cardiovascular risk.

**Fish** — loses quality and nutritional integrity when reheated.

## After Cooking

- Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours of cooking or removing from heat
- Do not leave food in the temperature danger zone (4–60°C) for extended periods
- Wrap or seal all leftovers to prevent bacterial growth and odor transfer
- For large volumes: divide into smaller containers to cool and refrigerate faster
- Poor refrigeration practices are linked to increased food-related digestive illness
- 50–87% of foodborne illnesses are linked to food prepared at home
